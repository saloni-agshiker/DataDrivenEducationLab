Dr. Ashok Goel's Talk from 3/3/21 

1. Knowledge-Based AI class - online vs. in person classes & engagement

   - See "An experiment in teaching cognitive systems online" (Goel & Joyner, 2015) (https://smartech.gatech.edu/handle/1853/53704)

2. AI TA Jill Watson

   - For a summary and references, see: http://dilab.gatech.edu/a-suite-of-online-learning-tools/
   - Answers questions on Piazza, and is trained on what TA responses in prior classes
   - Goal is to reduce workload for the instructor and allow the instructor to focus on deeper questions
   - Gender bias issues - A male student posted "My wife is pregnant and I'll need to take a few weeks off...". Jill responded "Welcome to the class, and welcome to the new addition to your family". When a female student posted "I'm pregnant and I'll need to take a few weeks off...", Jill only said "Welcome to the class". (These quotes aren't exact.) This is because the gender ratio was/is quite skewed in these classes, so there was past data of a male student making a similar comment, to which a TA replied "welcome to the new addition to your family". So Jill said that to the man but not the pregnant woman. (See "Jill Watson Doesn't Care if You're Pregnant: Grounding AI Ethics in Empirical Studies" in the references)
   - New version of teaching assistant doesn't use responses from old classes, instead uses information from syllabus and reference materials
   - Agent Smith - a way for teachers with less time/expertise to create their own Jill Watsons

3. Jill Watson Social Agent - algorithm that helps students in large classes find people with similar interests

   - Also see http://dilab.gatech.edu/a-suite-of-online-learning-tools/

   - Students in the course are asked to introduce themselves. The system looks at their background & interests, and with the student's consent, informs people of comment interests so they can connect.
   - Students had a really positive view of this system - they were willing to sacrifice a little bit of privacy for the social interaction.
